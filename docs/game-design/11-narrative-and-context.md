# Narrative and historical context

Audience: narrative designers, writers, historians and interface designers. Purpose: define tone, setting and how the game presents historical context. Status: draft; theater, scenario history and interpretation need research.

## Narrative approach

Tankwar has no scripted protagonist or campaign in the initial design. The story of each fight emerges from its recorded decisions, crew limitations, movement and outcomes. Human viewers can reconstruct those decisions from the replay timeline rather than a fictional narration claiming access to an agent's private reasoning. Agent implementation, prompt and internal reasoning remain outside the game's knowledge.

The first proposed scenario is a fictional rural village in a WWII-era European setting. Fictional locations, matchmaking across historically incompatible factions or dates, mirror matches and other competitive abstractions must be labeled. Do not name a real battle or historical formation without evidence, context and a decision to depict it. A fictional matchup should not imply that the exact event occurred.

## Voice and presentation

Use clear, neutral descriptions: vehicle and variant, date where verified, scenario, crew report, event and outcome. Keep source-derived details distinct from game mechanics and uncertainty. Do not invent quotations, personal histories, crew identities, national stereotypes or first-person testimony. Provide sources and context for historical claims in vehicle and scenario reference pages, and allow corrections to be recorded.

## Boundaries and accessibility

Combat involves crew injury and destruction; depict outcomes with legible status and restrained audiovisual cues rather than gratuitous injury. The initial plan does not require human character models or dialogue. Symbols, markings, localization and depictions of actual military organizations require a separate context and distribution review; do not infer their appropriateness from a model's appearance. Narrative descriptions, captions and event summaries must remain readable without sound or motion.

## Acceptance

Each released scenario identifies whether it is fictional or historical, cites its context and discloses meaningful competitive abstractions. Every vehicle page names its exact sourced configuration. Replay summaries match recorded events and avoid claims about agent motives. Review text for accuracy, context and accessibility before publishing.
